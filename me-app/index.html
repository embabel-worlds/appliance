Appliance

Ambient

The second one asks macOS for permission the first time it reads each app ("Embabel Me wants to control Google Chrome"). macOS asks only once — if you decline, re-enable it in System Settings. The last two let your assistant reach this machine when you ask it to ("do I have a tab open with…", "copy that"). It can only ever ask for things this app publishes — never run code here — and answers carry the least it can: tab titles and sites, never full URLs.

Scan

Local files

Share folders — Documents, a projects directory — with your appliance. Each appears read-only inside the assistant container under /local, and Apply wires it into the assistant's virtual Cypher: "what changed this week?", "which files mention the renewal?" are answered by walking your folders live — nothing is copied or stored. Tick index contents to go further for a folder: its documents (PDFs included) are embedded into the knowledge base, for summarization and semantic search. Applying rewrites docker-compose.override.yml and restarts the assistant container; the graph and everything it remembers stay up.

Ask your documents

Answered from the documents your appliance has ingested — shared folders you ticked to index, uploads, pages you saved. Every claim carries a citation you can open: local files reveal in Finder, web sources open where they came from.

Dates are the document's own — when the file was last changed or created — not when your appliance happened to index it. A document whose source never carried that date is excluded by a date filter rather than guessed at.

Models

Which model does what. Models running on this Mac — LM Studio, Ollama — cost nothing to run and keep the work on your machine; hosted ones are billed to your own provider key. A change takes effect on the next piece of work: no restart. Starting a NEW local model is the exception — the appliance registers models at boot, so restart it to pick one up.
